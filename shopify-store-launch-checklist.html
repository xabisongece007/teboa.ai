Launch Checklist

Launch cleaner, not just faster.

Before your Shopify store goes live, make sure the basics are solid so traffic does not hit an unfinished backend.

Open Teboa

Why stores struggle after launch

Many Shopify launches focus hard on theme design and product uploads, then leave the operational basics half-finished. That is where avoidable problems show up: broken payment setup, missing shipping logic, weak customer notifications, poor support readiness, or no analytics to measure what is happening. This checklist is built to stop that.

Simple rule: If you have not tested it end to end, do not assume it is ready just because the storefront looks complete.

The eight launch checks

1. Connect your custom domain

Make sure your domain resolves correctly, SSL is active, and the storefront loads properly on both desktop and mobile.

2. Confirm payments, currency, tax, and shipping

Check that your payment gateways work, currency is correct, tax settings are appropriate, and local shipping zones and rates are set properly.

3. Clean up your product structure

Products should sit in sensible collections with complete titles, pricing, descriptions, variants, and images. Messy catalog structure creates confusion immediately.

4. Test the full checkout flow

Run a real end-to-end test from product page to order confirmation. Check cart behavior, checkout fields, order emails, and confirmation messaging.

5. Review customer notifications

Order confirmations, shipping updates, and support responses should all sound clear and trustworthy. Broken or generic messaging weakens confidence fast.

6. Keep the app stack lean

Only install the apps you actually need. Too many overlapping apps create performance drag, extra cost, and unnecessary complexity before the store has even gained traction.

7. Prepare support channels

Customers need a clear way to reach you. Make sure email, WhatsApp, help content, or your support channel is live before the first traffic push.

8. Track analytics from day one

Install and verify your analytics stack so you can measure visits, add-to-carts, checkouts, and conversions immediately after launch.

What a ready launch actually looks like

A ready store is not just attractive. It is operationally usable. Customers can browse cleanly, pay without friction, receive the right messages, and get support if something goes wrong. On your side, you should be able to see performance, process orders, and respond without scrambling.
